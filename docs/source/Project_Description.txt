1. Overview

1.1 What you are building
An interactive system that helps a student choose a project. It collects startup ideas, products and niches
from public sources (YC directory, Hacker News, GitHub, Product Hunt or Reddit), organises them into a map
of niches and trends, and works in two ways:
      Discover. The student gives a profile (skills, hours, team size, compute, goal: startup, portfolio or
      course project). The system proposes ideas and niches that fit, including new ideas built from gaps it
      found in the data.
      Validate. The student brings an idea (or picks one the system proposed). The system returns a verdict,
      GO, PIVOT, NO-GO or INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE, with the evidence behind it, what would change it,
      and how stable it is.
It is a complete product: web interface, multi-agent backend, knowledge base (RAG), a fine-tuned scoring
model, monitoring and evaluation of the agents (AgenticOps), and a delivery pipeline that builds, tests and
deploys everything on Kubernetes.

5. How the verdict is decided
Principle. The language model never outputs the verdict. Models produce evidence-backed scores, and a
deterministic, unit-tested program (M11) turns them into the verdict. This makes it explainable, repeatable
and testable. It is decision support, not a prediction of success.
Why this design. Idea-validation frameworks list dimensions but give no formula, so your team defines
and defends one. Weighted scoring models are standard in project selection, but their weights are
subjective and close totals flip with small changes, so the engine also reports how stable the verdict is.
Research on LLM judges shows that scores drift unless the rubric is locked, evidence is required and scores
are calibrated against human labels, so every score carries evidence ids and uses a versioned rubric.

5.1 Six dimensions
Each is scored 0 to 5 (higher is better for the student) with a confidence from 0 to 1 and evidence ids. Each
score combines computed features (numbers calculated in code from the knowledge base) with the fine-
tuned model's judgment, under a versioned rubric.

 Dimension                             5 means
 Demand and pain                       Many independent sources show the same strong, recurring problem.
 Competition                           Few or weak direct competitors, so there is room to enter.
 Differentiation                       A clear gap that existing products do not address.
 Feasibility for this student          Fits the student's hours, skills and compute with margin.
 Risk                                  No legal, privacy, safety or platform-dependency concern.
 Learning and portfolio value Teaches skills the student wants and shows well on a CV.

5.3 Score, thresholds and stability
      Weighted score. Sum of weight x score, scaled to 0 to 100. Weights come from the goal and add to
      100. Starting presets (you may change them and must justify it):

 Goal                                  Demand                         Compet.        Differ.                             Feasib.                  Risk                          Learn.
 Startup                               25                             20             20                                  15                       15                            5
 Portfolio                             15                             15             15                                  25                       10                            20
 Course project                        10                             10             15                                  35                       10                            20

      Thresholds. GO needs a score of at least T_go and no score below the floor on the first five
      dimensions. A score between T_no and T_go, or a floor breach, gives PIVOT and names the weakest
      dimension. Below T_no is NO-GO. Starting values: T_go 70, T_no 50, floor 1.5. You tune them (Guide
      Section 10).
      Confidence. 0.7 x mean dimension confidence + 0.3 x min(1, sources / 5). Below 0.5 the verdict
      becomes INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE: the system abstains instead of guessing.
      Challenge. The Skeptic argues the opposite. Each objection the critic accepts as evidence-backed
      lowers the matching score by 0.5 (at most 1.0) and its confidence by 0.1. Unsupported objections are
      discarded.
      Sensitivity. The engine recomputes the verdict with each weight moved up and down by 20 percent
      (the difference shared among the others) and the least confident score moved by one point. If any run
      changes the verdict, the card says BORDERLINE and shows what flips it.
      What would make this GO. The engine finds the smallest change that changes the verdict. Applying
      it re-runs the scorers and the engine and creates a new version.

5.4 Worked example
Goal Portfolio (weights 15 / 15 / 15 / 25 / 10 / 20). Scores: Demand 3.5, Competition 2.0, Differentiation 3.0,
Feasibility 4.0, Risk 3.5, Learning 4.5. All gates pass.
  1. Weighted sum = 52.5 + 30 + 45 + 100 + 35 + 90 = 352.5. Divide by 5: 70.5. That is above T_go = 70
     and no floor is breached, so the first result is GO.
  2. Lower the Feasibility weight by 20 percent (25 to 20) and share the 5 freed points over the others in
     proportion. The score becomes about 69.9, so the verdict flips to PIVOT and the card says GO,
     BORDERLINE.
  3. What would make this GO: raise Competition from 2.0 to 3.0 (for example by narrowing to a less
     crowded segment). The score becomes 73.5, and it stays above 70 under the same weight change
     (about 73.1), so it is a stable GO.
